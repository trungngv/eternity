## Lessons

### Positioning defines context, not messaging
> "Positioning defines how your product is the best in the world at delivering something, some value that a well-defined set of customers cares a lot about."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=2s)

Positioning is fundamentally about setting the context in which customers understand your product—what competitors they compare you against, what price they expect to pay, and where they buy it. It's the foundation for all marketing and sales, not just a tagline or messaging exercise.

### Reframe the same product, change everything
> "I can choose to position it as a muffin. It's the same product, but the assumptions about that, the competitors are different, your value is different, the assumptions that customers have about that product are different because I've placed it in a different context."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=245s)

The same physical product positioned differently (e.g., as cake vs. muffin) creates entirely different customer perceptions about competitors, pricing, and distribution. Context shapes how customers evaluate your offering.

### Target underserved subsegments, not market leaders
> "The most common way that tech companies successfully position is they find an underserved subsegment of the market and then they attempt to dominate that subsegment and then push out from there."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=10s)

Successful tech companies rarely win by competing head-to-head with established leaders. Instead, they find underserved niches where they can be the best, dominate that segment, and expand from a position of strength.

### Fear dominates B2B decision-making
> "In business-to-business, the granddaddy of emotions is fear. Fear making a poor choice, fear looking bad in front of my boss, fear getting fired. Like, fear of making a mistake drives a lot of decisions in B2B."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=21s)

Unlike consumer marketing driven by diverse emotions, B2B buying is fundamentally driven by fear—fear of making a bad choice, looking bad to leadership, or getting fired. Understanding and addressing this fear is critical to B2B positioning and sales.

### Positioning requires cross-functional alignment
> "In the work that I do with companies with positioning, we do it with a cross-functional team. So, we bring together product, marketing, sales, customer success, support."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=45s)

Positioning cannot be a marketing-only exercise. It requires input and buy-in from product, sales, customer success, support, and the CEO. Without cross-functional agreement, the positioning won't stick in execution.

### Define competitive alternatives, not just features
> "The component pieces of positioning are competitive alternatives. So, if you didn't exist, what would a customer do? And then we need to understand what makes us different, which is our differentiated capabilities."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=33s)

The foundation of positioning is understanding what customers would do if you didn't exist. Then identify what unique value you deliver that alternatives don't, and determine which customers care most about that specific value.

### B2B value comes down to money: making or saving it
> "In businesses, I mean, we kind of only have two things. We're either helping you make money or we're helping you save money, and that's about it."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=20s)

In B2B, the value proposition is constrained compared to consumer products. Businesses only care about two things: making money or saving money. This focus determines what positioning will resonate.

### No decision is your biggest competitor
> "40 to 60% of B2B purchase processes end in no decision... It's because we look at our choices, we can't figure out how to do that in a way that we're sure isn't going to get us into trouble."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=68s)

Research on millions of B2B sales calls shows 40-60% of deals end in 'no decision'—not because competitors win, but because buyers can't confidently decide without fear of consequences. Reducing uncertainty is more important than beating competitors.

### Avoid creating fake categories
> "Lately what I've seen is companies that love the idea of category creation are attempting to create a category when they obviously fit in an existing market category and it's kind of wishful thinking that this is a new category."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=62s)

A common positioning mistake is trying to invent a new market category when you obviously fit in an existing one. This confuses customers and makes selling harder. Stay in existing categories when appropriate, only create new ones when truly innovative.

### B2B storytelling must frame market alternatives
> "In that storytelling framework, we need to have a spot in that framework to paint a picture of the whole market and then show where we fit and where everybody fits. We shouldn't actually be just talking about us."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vM_1G1LCotU&t=51s)

B2B storytelling should help customers understand the full landscape of alternatives—different approaches, tradeoffs, and why your solution is the best fit. This reduces buyer indecision by making the choice feel safe and well-reasoned.

# Dr. Julie Gurner: Standards, Rules, and Curating Friendships
**Source:** [Watch on YouTube](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y)
**Date:** --

## Lessons

### High Standards Drive Leadership Distinction
> "the standards you hold for yourself is high and it is why you are sitting in your seat and other people if they held that exact same standard would not be in their seat"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=120s)

Successful leaders hold themselves to higher standards than others, which is why they're in their positions. This creates natural differentiation and attracts ambitious teams, but it requires understanding that others won't share your obsession while still expecting professional excellence.

### Detach Self-Worth from Business Results
> "if it's too close to you it's going to change how you operate it's not going to give you the greatest objectivity"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=420s)

When you tie your identity too closely to your business outcomes, it clouds your judgment and prevents objective decision-making. Healthy separation between who you are and what you do enables better strategic choices and resilience through challenges.

### Seek Outside Perspective to Uncover Blind Spots
> "you have to go outside of yourself sometimes and be open so sometimes I'll do things like 360s for people where you know they'll trust me"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=540s)

You cannot see your own blind spots from within your own perspective. Conducting 360-degree feedback with diverse voices—direct reports, peers, board members, and outsiders—reveals patterns you're unable to see yourself.

### Care Deeply to Challenge Directly
> "you have to care deeply to be able to challenge directly and that somebody has to know that you care deeply about who they are in their role"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=720s)

Radical candor requires genuine care for someone's growth and success. People can receive hard feedback when they know it comes from someone in their corner who wants them to win, not from someone trying to punish or dominate.

### Collaborate Against Problems, Not Each Other
> "rather than me and then you are the problem it is I'm pulling you to my side of the table and it's us together versus this problem"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=1260s)

Hard conversations are less defensive and more productive when you shift the frame from confrontation to collaboration. Instead of positioning it as you versus them, pull them to your side as partners against a shared problem.

### Choose Your Emotional Response Intentionally
> "you have to make a decision whether like is was this a bad 10 minutes or was it a bad hour or is it going to be a bad day right"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=1380s)

You control whether a bad moment becomes a bad day by making a conscious decision about how to move forward. Leaders who can reset their emotions maintain effectiveness and prevent their mood from infecting their team.

### Set Boundaries to Protect Your Capacity
> "usually people are pretty okay taking that hint and they you know fade away a bit and then eventually this thing kind of falls off"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=1620s)

Direct communication about your limitations and focus areas allows you to gracefully exit relationships that no longer serve either party. Most people accept clear boundaries when you're honest about what you can and cannot offer.

### Address Issues Early to Prevent Escalation
> "having the conversations early having the collaborations rather than conflict early addressing problems early saves you so much on the back end that you can never predict"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=1860s)

Problems compound exponentially over time. Intervening early with a potential difficult employee or relationship issue saves months of damage, protecting your metrics, brand, and emotional energy compared to letting issues fester.

### Import Ideas from Outside Your Field
> "people who are exceptional are pulling in like you know from outside of their fields outside of their way of thinking outside of tradition"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=2100s)

Exceptional performers break cognitive ruts by actively pulling insights from diverse fields and traditions. Great marketers study art, great executives read philosophy—cross-pollination of ideas creates competitive advantage.

### Shift Perspective to See Beyond Your System
> "it's really hard to understand a system that you're a part of when all you see is that system and so it's really interesting blind spots again right how do we get rid of blind spots we change our perspective we change our lens into the situation"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iTGZg6GYc0Y&t=2460s)

You cannot fully understand a system you're embedded in because you only see that one system. Changing your perspective and lens on a situation reveals insights and options that were previously invisible.

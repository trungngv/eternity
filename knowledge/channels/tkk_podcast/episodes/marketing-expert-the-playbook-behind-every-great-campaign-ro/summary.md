## Lessons

### Humans Choose by Comparing, Not Optimizing
> "you'll have to show them three or four or five skiing holidays from which they choose because we can't really choose in the absence of comparison"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=QBznUHAopxU&t=120s)

People cannot make meaningful decisions in isolation — they need alternatives to evaluate against. This has profound implications for AI design and product interfaces, which risk presenting a single 'best' answer rather than a set of options to compare.

### Efficiency Focus Disregards Psychology, Where Real Gains Hide
> "you focus too heavily on cost reduction and too little on value creation"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=QBznUHAopxU&t=240s)

When organizations chase efficiency, they measure mechanical and numerical factors while ignoring psychological ones — which often yield far greater returns. The result is cost-cutting that quietly destroys value.

### One Human Interaction Outweighs All Operational Metrics
> "the major determinant of whether you liked royal male or not was whether you liked your postman or posty"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=QBznUHAopxU&t=480s)

Royal Mail found that brand perception had nothing to do with delivery reliability — it depended entirely on whether customers liked their postman. A single warm human touchpoint can override years of operational investment.

### Trust in the Seller Substitutes for Unknowable Product Quality
> "I'm going to ask a supplementary heuristic question which is do I trust the person who's selling the car to me?"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=QBznUHAopxU&t=660s)

When buyers lack the expertise to evaluate a product directly, they unconsciously substitute a simpler question: do I trust this person? This is an evolutionarily rational heuristic, not irrationality — humans have half a million years of practice judging people, not postal efficiency.

### Short-Term Metrics Structurally Undermine Long-Term Marketing
> "the requirement for short-term self-justification and numbers effectively overrides the real purpose of marketing which is investment in long-term customer value"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=QBznUHAopxU&t=1800s)

Publicly traded companies are incentivized to justify spend with short-term numbers, which crowds out marketing's true purpose: building durable customer relationships. Private and founder-led companies escape this structural trap and consistently outperform on brand.

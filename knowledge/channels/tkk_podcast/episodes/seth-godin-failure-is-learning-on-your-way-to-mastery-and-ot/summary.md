# Seth Godin: "Failure is Learning on Your Way to Mastery" and Other Advice for Creativity
**Source:** [Watch on YouTube](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY)
**Date:** --

## Lessons

### The Internet Reaches Specific People, Not Everyone
> "The internet is a micro medium. It is the best medium ever developed to reach specific people, but it is terrible at reaching everyone."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY&t=0s)

The internet is a micro-medium designed to reach specific audiences deeply, not a mass medium for reaching everyone. Understanding this fundamentally changes how you approach building, marketing, and serving your work.

### Process Protects You from Fluctuating Emotions and Doubt
> "If you have a practice, you get through them because you already decided there was going to be a blog post tomorrow, and you already decided that the podcast comes out on a regular basis."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY&t=61s)

By establishing a consistent practice, you create a container that carries you through bad moods, criticism, and doubt. The key is deciding once, then executing the practice forever without re-negotiating.

### Build for Your Smallest Viable Audience, Not Everyone
> "The smallest viable audience, not the biggest possible audience. We have to be for someone. The internet is not a mass medium."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY&t=540s)

Focus intensely on who your work IS for rather than who it's not for. This specificity attracts the right people and prevents creating mediocre work that appeals to nobody. Harry Potter is for precocious 12-year-olds, not everyone.

### Failure is Learning on Your Way to Mastery
> "Learning is serial incompetence on our way to getting better. The only way to learn is by doing things... learning is the act of failing on our way to mastery."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY&t=1017s)

Real learning is 'serial incompetence' — you must fail repeatedly to improve. Organized education prevents failure and growth; creative work demands you embrace it as the path forward.

### Creativity Requires Choosing to Exert Emotional Labor
> "If you exert the emotional labor to do those two things, sometimes you will have a successful creative outcome. That's a choice."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY&t=3417s)

Creativity is not about waiting for inspiration—it's a deliberate choice to extend yourself with empathy and announce uncertain outcomes. The emotional labor of showing up, being vulnerable, and taking creative risks is what enables creative breakthroughs.

### Meet Spec and Ship; Perfectionism is a Form of Hiding
> "Quality is just one thing. It meets spec. Good enough is not a slur. Good enough is a definition. It met spec."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY&t=3783s)

Quality means meeting the spec you defined, not achieving perfection. Once work meets your spec, ship it. Perfectionism is a socially acceptable way to avoid the vulnerability of putting work into the world.

### Professionals Keep Promises Regardless of How They Feel
> "A professional makes a promise and then keeps the promise whether or not they feel like it. Amateurs get to be authentic. Amateurs show up when they want to."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY&t=3600s)

Amateurs show up when inspiration strikes; professionals show up because they made a promise. This consistency—not authenticity or passion—is what builds real creative work and separates professionals from hobbyists.

### Stop Shaming Yourself and Others—It Extinguishes Humanity
> "Shame has been sharpened into a tool for cultural coercion and compliance... it extinguishes part of our humanity when we shame people."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=gX-n8I2PAdY&t=4080s)

Shame has been weaponized as a tool for cultural control and compliance. Both external shaming from others and internal self-shaming destroy the conditions needed for creative work, growth, and human flourishing.

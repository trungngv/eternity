## Lessons

### Three Questions Reveal Authentic Leadership
> "is this about you is it about I versus we is about me versus service are you about what we're trying to accomplish here this compelling vision for the organization or is it about you the second question is you're the leader where are we going"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=0s)

Every leader must ask themselves: Is this about serving the greater good or serving myself? Where am I taking this organization? Do I truly see and respect the people I'm leading? These questions form the foundation of authentic, servant-oriented leadership.

### Build an Integrated Life Across All Domains
> "develop one integrated life to deliver your life's work so my life's work is service and I'm going to integrate all of those components and people ask me a lot of times well how do you do that I said well you first of all you you decide what's you really important to you in each one of those circles"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=360s)

Success requires intentionally integrating work, family, spiritual life, and community contributions into one cohesive whole. Rather than compartmentalizing these areas, deliberately schedule and nurture each dimension daily, understanding that your life's work includes all of them.

### Service and Leadership Are Inseparable
> "the serve is to live honey thank you Dad that's great well now the serve is to live honey thank you Dad that's great and then and so I just work was always a service and it was just fabulous I felt good about it I wanted to be better at it"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=180s)

Leadership begins with adopting a service mindset from the earliest stages of your career. By focusing on serving others—whether as a grocery store employee or a CEO—you develop the character and humility necessary to lead organizations effectively and earn people's genuine commitment.

### Clear Expected Behaviors Create Culture and Safety
> "a proposing a plan being positive having to find a way attitude uh respect in each other uh listen to each other help each other appreciate each other you can see very sophisticated words shame very sophisticated but we can see this and we can help each other in our personal development plans"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=1200s)

Organizations need explicit, codified expected behaviors (respect, listening, helping, appreciating each other) enforced with zero tolerance for violations. When people know exactly what's expected, they feel psychologically safe to contribute fully and hold themselves accountable.

### Celebrate Problems as Opportunities to Improve
> "red item is a gem we I clap when you have it read because you're not the you're not red it's your items red thanks for sharing that now we all can work together to help you and us turn the red seals green"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=1140s)

Creating a culture where people openly share red items (problems) without fear requires reframing issues as valuable data that enable the team to improve together. When leaders visibly appreciate and clap for bad news, it signals that transparency trumps appearance.

### Presence and Full Attention Demonstrate Respect
> "and I'm going to see you means that I respect you I care about you I'm interested in you I want to thank you because we're going to do this together and together we're gonna we're gonna create a lot of value for the greater good it's almost like a presence feeling right you can tell when people are present in the conversation"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=2700s)

Truly seeing and respecting others means giving them your complete presence—eye contact, undivided attention, and genuine interest. When you're not fully present (checking phones, distracted), people feel it and withdraw their commitment to shared goals.

### Weekly Business Reviews Align Organization and Reality
> "we pull together on the on the business plan we updated the crazy value roadmap tit to to restructure ourselves to match our production of the real demand and develop a competitive cost structure at the same time we committed to develop a new product line"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=1440s)

Consistent, disciplined weekly business plan reviews that include all key stakeholders create shared understanding of strategy, current status (with color-coded metrics), and issues requiring attention. This reliable process keeps everyone on the same page and enables rapid problem-solving.

### Consistency and Discipline Transform Behavior Into Character
> "the discipline of following the process and also to your question for your comment the discipline of the behaviors see if somebody would go after somebody or act like they knew what the answer was or be rhetorical questions or whatever anything that would cause an unsafe culture of Love by Design where people felt like they're going to be a victim or be made fun of then their commitment to sharing everything is going to go down"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=1980s)

Sustained, consistent application of expected behaviors gradually transforms how people think and what they believe. Over time, repeatedly acting with respect, humility, and service shifts not just actions but underlying values—you become what you practice.

### Teach Feedback as Vulnerability, Not Criticism
> "I think we all can learn from that but do you remember rather that you were going to pick me up and you're going to take me to the my event and I just want you to know that I know you care about me I know you love me you are very reliable and I just want you to know I think that might be an opportunity to learn"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=4320s)

Help others communicate difficult truths by teaching them to express what they care about and how they felt when commitments were broken, rather than attacking the person. This approach preserves dignity and invites genuine accountability without defensive reactions.

### Humility Compounds Leadership Impact Across Generations
> "every one of them is running their version of a family meeting a business plan review every week because it's the very same ones the kids uh the other principles and practices that we just went everybody's gonna get a chance to see that was on his desk right in front of me"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=2tOWvdWSZzk&t=4560s)

Leaders who embody humility, admit mistakes, apologize sincerely, and model continuous improvement inspire others to adopt the same mindset. These principles proven effective in organizations scale naturally to families and communities, creating multi-generational impact.

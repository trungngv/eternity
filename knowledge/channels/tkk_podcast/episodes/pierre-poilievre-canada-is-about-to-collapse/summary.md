# Pierre Poilievre: Canada Is About to Collapse

## Lessons

### Government Should Only Do What People Cannot
> "government should only do what the people can't do for themselves and then leave the rest to free people uh to decide how to live."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=Go5wjr20mTs&t=180s)

Poilievre argues that because government is uniquely defined by its legal monopoly on force, it should restrict itself to functions that genuinely require collective coercion—defense, policing, basic infrastructure, and a safety net. Expanding beyond that crowds out individual freedom and private initiative.

### Growth Without Capacity Creates Unavoidable Shortages
> "when your population is growing faster than health care, housing, and jobs, then eventually you're going to have shortages."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=Go5wjr20mTs&t=480s)

When population expands faster than housing, healthcare, and jobs, scarcity is a mathematical certainty rather than an isolated policy failure. This principle applies to any system—organizations, cities, or nations—where headcount outpaces supporting infrastructure.

### Hope Is a Strategy, Not Just a Feeling
> "My purpose is to provide people with hope, and that's not just a touchy-feely word. It's actually a political strategy for me. Because if people can be convinced that there's hope then they'll vote for it in election."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=Go5wjr20mTs&t=840s)

Poilievre reframes hope as a deliberate leadership tool: if people believe improvement is possible, they act and vote accordingly. Allowing constituents to fall into hopelessness cedes the field to the status quo by default.

### Dependency and Independence Cannot Coexist
> "Can something that is dependent be independent?"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=Go5wjr20mTs&t=1980s)

Using media as the example, Poilievre makes a first-principles argument: an entity that relies on government funding cannot, by definition, hold government accountable. The same logic applies to any watchdog or oversight body funded by the very entity it is meant to scrutinize.

### Focus on Controllables to Eliminate Worry
> "I focus on what I can control. And that is the most liberating thing you can do."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=Go5wjr20mTs&t=2820s)

Poilievre describes how growing exposure to stress paradoxically reduced his anxiety by forcing a realization: worrying about uncontrollable events serves no constructive purpose. Channeling energy exclusively into what is within one's control is both a stress-management practice and a source of personal freedom.

## Lessons

### Micro Understanding Before Macro Decision-Making
> "What I was focused on is micro understanding. Because if you don't understand the business down to where the rubber meets the road, you can make decisions at the top which are not implementable."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=15s)

Understanding the business at the granular, operational level is essential for making implementable decisions at the top. Without deep comprehension of where "the rubber meets the road," executives make strategic decisions that can't actually be executed by frontline teams.

### Zoom In Before You Zoom Out
> "My philosophy was zoom in before you zoom out. So in everything I've done I always went deep into whatever the business was the company was try to understand things from the ground level then I'd zoom out"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=900s)

Master the details of any business first, then pull back to see the strategic picture. This approach, repeated throughout a career, creates leaders who understand both minutiae and vision, making them capable of bridging top-level strategy with operational reality.

### Humility and Balance Trump Title and Power
> "You may be the president of whatever of PepsiCo or whatever, but you come home. You're a wife, a mother, and a daughter. Nobody can take your place. So leave that crown in the garage."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=1920s)

Success requires recognizing that professional titles and organizational power don't diminish the importance of personal roles—as a spouse, parent, family member. Maintaining this perspective prevents the ego inflation that often accompanies leadership and keeps leaders grounded.

### Show Passion, Give Credit to Teams
> "If the team does a great job at the end of it, even though you were the program manager and you drove it, give the credit to the team because you couldn't have done it yourself. The team did it."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=4320s)

Passion and high standards drive excellence, but leaders must direct credit downward. Taking credit yourself erodes trust and morale; attributing success to the team builds loyalty and inspires continued effort, while leaders absorb blame when things fail.

### Adapt Strategy When Market Conditions Change
> "You change strategy, the environment changes. You cannot be dogmatic about your strategic direction. When the environment changes around you, you've got to zag a little bit."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=5040s)

Strategic decisions must evolve with environmental changes. Being dogmatic about direction, even successful ones, leads to failure when circumstances shift. Leaders must have the humility to change course and the courage to make large bets when markets require it.

### Understand Organizational Politics Without Playing Them
> "you have to understand the politics, but don't play in the politics. Don't meddle in the politics. Just understand it and then figure out how best to contribute within that."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=1740s)

Awareness of organizational dynamics and power relationships is necessary for effectiveness, but active participation in politics—gossip, meddling, coalition-building—becomes destructive. Stay focused on the work and company mission instead.

### Decisions Combine Data, Experience, and Counsel
> "decisions are made because you've looked at dreams of data. You're married with experience with a little bit of intuition, a lot of counseling with other people, a lot of discussions, a lot of input from other people too."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=5100s)

Strong decision-making integrates multiple sources: quantitative data, personal experience, intuition, and input from diverse perspectives. Leaders who synthesize these elements make better choices than those who rely on any single source of information.

### Take Calculated Risks for Personal Growth
> "I knew one was risky and one was safe. And u something in me wanted to take that risky bet also."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=480s)

Choosing the safe path often means missing transformative opportunities. When faced with a secure but limiting choice versus a risky but growth-oriented one, exceptional leaders choose the calculated risk, especially when timing may not come again.

### Develop Future Leaders Systematically
> "There's about three or 400 that were corporate assets and one watch them all the time because these are people who in 15 years could be CEO."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=1620s)

Identify high-potential leaders early (those with brilliant, challenging ideas who prioritize company over self) and monitor their development closely. Give them diverse assignments and stretch opportunities, even if lateral moves are necessary, to build a deep bench of future CEOs.

### Address Bias and Bad Behavior Immediately
> "I would call out bad behavior immediately when I saw it. but in a gentle way. I'd say, 'Hey, can you let her finish? Stop interrupting her.'"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=E50ix2Y62y4&t=4140s)

Unconscious biases erode confidence and competence in underrepresented groups. Leaders must call out discriminatory or exclusionary behavior in the moment, gently but directly, to create an environment where all talent can thrive and contribute fully.

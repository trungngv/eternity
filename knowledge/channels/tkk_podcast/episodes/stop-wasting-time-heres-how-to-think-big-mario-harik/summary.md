## Lessons

### Apply engineering problem-solving to business decisions
> "The engineering design process, it's based on one identifying a problem or a goal. Then it's about collecting a lot of data around that particular problem or goal. Then defining your requirements. Then designing and building a solution and then eventually testing it for what the outcome would look like."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=1s)

An engineering mindset provides a powerful framework for business: identify problems, collect data, define requirements, design solutions, and test outcomes. This disciplined approach to decision-making creates consistency and rational thinking across all business challenges.

### Balance engineering discipline with genuine people leadership
> "How you can transform or translate that framework and how you manage people and love people and believe in them and believe what's in best in them is the other ingredient to be able to then enable you to deliver good outcomes."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=180s)

Engineering thinking alone leads to perfectionism that frustrates teams. True leadership requires coupling analytical discipline with empathy—believing in people's potential, understanding their individuality, and helping them become their best selves rather than forcing them into rigid systems.

### Set ultra-big goals to prevent complacency
> "Always think big. Don't set small goal. Life is short. Set big goals. Because when you set big goals, you achieve great things. If you set small goals, you achieve small small things."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=420s)

Small goals breed satisfaction and stagnation. Life is short—set ambitious goals in business, family, and personal pursuits that stretch your capabilities. Big goals create the momentum where winning breeds more winning and keeps your team energized.

### Hire three attributes: excellence, dedication, and collegiality
> "Are they good at what they do or do they have a high intellect? Number two is are they serious about work? And the third one is are you collegial? Are you somebody who gets along with the rest of the team who try to look for what's best in the team?"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=480s)

Build teams by screening for three dimensions: competence and passion in their field, seriousness about maximizing daily output, and collegiality—the ability to uplift teammates with kindness, humility, and willingness to learn. This combination creates high-performing cultures.

### Seek disagreement—consensus often masks incomplete analysis
> "Usually I worry if I'm in a team meeting and everybody is aligned on a solution and the reason why because the first question I ask myself did we look at that problem or that solution in 10 different ways to then have different perspectives of how to solve that problem."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=660s)

Be suspicious when teams instantly align on solutions; it suggests insufficient exploration. Encourage respectful disagreement informed by data and diverse perspectives. The friction of rigorous debate, followed by unified commitment, leads to better outcomes.

### Obsess daily over KPIs and their rate of change
> "Every single day, there's approximately 10 KPIs that we monitor. And for each one of those KPIs, not only we look at the first derivative change of that KPI, but also what the second derivative looks like, which is what's the slope? What's the rate of change?"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=1020s)

Monitor 10 core KPIs daily, but don't just track their absolute value—measure the second derivative (rate of change). This reveals whether metrics are accelerating or decelerating, enabling you to intervene quickly with targeted action plans before problems compound.

### Go to the field—KPIs don't reveal human insights
> "I spend a lot of time in the field. I actually like to go to our docks to our terminals where I get to sit with our drivers and dock workers and usually I go there early in the morning and I sit in the break room and I sit with our drivers and I tell them tell me what's going on in the business."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=1140s)

Spend time with frontline employees in terminals and break rooms to learn what data misses: operational friction, compensation concerns, process improvements, and technology gaps. These conversations reveal where action plans need adjustment and where strategy should pivot.

### Make limited time meaningful rather than spreading thin
> "When I'm with my kids, for example, I don't use my phone. I put it away. I do meaningful activities with them. When you are limited on time, what you can focus on is making the quality of that time be the highest possible."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=1440s)

When juggling competing demands—leadership, family, learning—quality beats quantity. Put away distractions, engage fully in activities that matter, and be intentional about where you spend time. This focus yields more impact than trying to do everything.

### Success is others realizing their full potential
> "Success for me is someone including myself realizing their full potential. Is being able to achieve what they can achieve with the tools, education, background that they have been given and to be the best version of themselves while doing so also being happy and emotionally fulfilled."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=XCO7mqK7hs0&t=5220s)

True success transcends business metrics—it's seeing your kids, team, and colleagues become their best selves while remaining emotionally fulfilled and happy. Growth divorced from well-being isn't worth achieving; impact that enriches lives is the ultimate measure.

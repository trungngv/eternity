## Lessons

### First dates are about experience, not evaluation
> "imagine that you spend 50 years with someone the day that you meet is 0.55% of your total time with them and so it's not about gathering information it's about having an experience with them"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=18s)

A first date represents only 0.55% of potential time together, making it crucial to focus on having an enjoyable shared experience rather than treating it as an interview to evaluate long-term compatibility. This mindset shift removes unnecessary pressure and allows genuine connection to develop naturally.

### The spark is often misleading about compatibility
> "only 11% of people feel love at first sight and that means many people are getting to know someone over time maybe they live in the same apartment building they work in the same place and that attraction and appreciation grows over time so the spark can definitely grow"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=180s)

Instant chemistry is not a reliable indicator of lasting relationship success; only 11% of people experience love at first sight, and many meaningful relationships develop gradually over time. Misconceptions about the spark cause people to dismiss compatible partners prematurely.

### Pursue slow burns over instant sparks
> "my antidote to the spark is going after the Slow Burn and the Slow Burn is somebody who you like them more and more as you get to know them they may not be instantly the most charismatic person that you've ever met but they're really a quality person"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=240s)

The most sustainable relationships often feature people you like progressively more as you get to know them, rather than immediate fireworks. These slow burns with quality people who grow on you provide more stable foundations than relationships built on initial attraction.

### Use the Post-Date 8 to assess compatibility
> "the postdate 8 is a series of questions that I came up with for a client who was having a hard time deciding if she wanted to see someone again and also somebody who was having a hard time spotting the slowburn so when you finish the date you look at this... how did I feel in my body during the day did I feel stiff did I feel relaxed"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=420s)

After a date, answer eight specific questions about how you felt physically, whether you laughed, felt heard, and crucially what side of yourself they brought out. This structured reflection helps identify genuine potential beyond initial chemistry and reveals what kind of partner you'll be with them.

### Date like a scientist: test your assumptions
> "date like a scientist which is this idea of have hypotheses so a hypothesis could be I need to date somebody with a graduate degree but then test that hypothesis maybe what you actually care about is intellectual curiosity and somebody who listens to the knowledge project"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=720s)

Challenge your dating preferences by testing whether they are genuine deal breakers or simply unchallenged assumptions. Many filters (like height requirements) eliminate compatible matches; testing them reveals what actually matters versus what you think should matter.

### What side of you they bring out matters most
> "whatever side of you you are with that person is who you'll be for a big majority of your life if you end up with that person and so the post8 8 is a great way to see when there's potential and to spot those slowburn people"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=480s)

The dynamic between you and a partner—which version of yourself you become around them—is more important than their individual qualities. You'll embody this version for most of your life together, making it critical to notice if someone brings out your best or worst self.

### Understand attachment styles to recognize patterns
> "there are people who are anxiously attached what happens for them is their story of love is that I'm going to chase you I have to convince you to like me you might pull away and we're going to have this Chase Dynamic and that I'm constantly worried that you're not going to be interested in me"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=1620s)

Anxious-avoidant dynamics create chase cycles that leave both partners unhappy; secure attachment enables healthy relationships. Recognizing your attachment style and choosing secure partners—or becoming secure yourself—breaks destructive patterns and builds stable relationships.

### Loyalty and emotional stability trump looks and money
> "the first one is loyalty really is this somebody who will stick by you of course that's a hard thing to find out on a first date but one proxy for that that I found is asking people about old friendships people who have friendships from different phases of their life"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=1320s)

Long-term relationship success depends more on someone's character—loyalty, how they handle adversity, and emotional stability—than on physical attractiveness or financial status. These foundational qualities predict whether a partner will remain committed and handle life's challenges together.

### Secure communication is more attractive than games
> "being clear about like hey that was a really great date I feel like we really connected I'd love to see you again and then like working together to come up with the next date"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=1860s)

Being clear about your interest and genuine appreciation—rather than playing it cool or waiting days to respond—attracts secure partners who value directness. Avoiding games and communicating authentically demonstrates the confidence and healthy attachment that builds strong relationships.

### Break up with honesty and empathy
> "there's this rule kind of in Corporate America that if you're going to lay somebody off or give them a bad performance review it shouldn't be the first time that they've gotten that feedback so I think it's much more fair to let somebody know throughout the relationship if something isn't working"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=q9CWf-y3vBo&t=3540s)

Respectful breakups require communicating concerns throughout the relationship rather than as a surprise, and respecting the other person's healing process. Delivering feedback during breakup primarily serves your guilt rather than helping them move forward.

## Lessons

### Trust Your Body's Intuitive Signals
> "George Soros famously said that when his back is acting up that he knows something is wrong in markets so people have a and even Einstein wrote about having a he said his uh talked about a kinesthetic awareness"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=120s)

Your body processes information and detects danger before your conscious mind recognizes it. This kinesthetic awareness evolved over millennia and should be refined and listened to in all decisions—not just physical safety, but investments, relationships, and negotiations.

### Never Make Decisions When Confused
> "one of my principles is um never make a decision when I'm confused when I'm confused now that like okay but that doesn't sound like such a big deal but I have made decisions when I was confused when I was confused or tired or stressed you and I would call it in in the stupid Zone"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=600s)

Decision-making while confused, tired, or stressed puts you in the 'stupid zone.' Pay attention to your feeling states in real-time to recognize when you're compromised and should defer critical decisions.

### Position Yourself from a Place of Strength
> "the best decision makers in the world almost always operate from a position of strength and that sort of strikes me because when you operate from a solid position or Foundation nearly every move you make is good it's all accumulative whereas when you operate from a poor position things almost always go from bad to worse"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=540s)

The best decision-makers operate from strong positions where nearly every move accumulates value. From weak positions, things deteriorate quickly. In chess and life, engineer situations toward positions where you excel rather than accepting unfavorable circumstances.

### Buffett's First Rule: Never Lose Money Early
> "because his his insight and it's a genius Insight is you can't help but make money over time just because human Enterprise that is learning and accumulating and growing so over time the value of any Enterprise of any business is going to grow um and so as long as you avoid loss um your money will grow"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=1560s)

Early losses in investing compound over time. If you make costly mistakes before becoming proficient, you'll have no capital remaining to invest when you're actually skilled. This makes the learning curve asymmetric—protect capital while learning.

### Ask the Question Behind the Question
> "a friend could come up to me and say uh Adam you know any good books on meditating but what's the question behind that why do you want a book I'm meditating oh because I want to give it to my teenage daughter because she's stressed in school"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=2400s)

Surface-level questions often miss the real problem. By asking 'why' to understand context and true intent, you discover root causes rather than symptoms, leading to elegant solutions that address actual needs.

### Accelerate Your Learning Cycles
> "so before I created my software if I wanted to test that question I have to download the data I'd have to get JPMorgan closing stock data for the last you know 40 odd years then I'd have to think of all the things I wanted to test it against... it took me about an hour to set all that up... I paid some programmers to hard code what I normally do using Excel so I can answer that question in about five seconds now"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=3240s)

The speed of learning is proportional to the frequency of feedback loops. By reducing friction between questions and answers—from hours to milliseconds—you dramatically accelerate mastery. Organize your work and practice to maximize feedback iterations.

### Use AI as a Thought Partner, Not an Oracle
> "the way to think about it is um is that it it's a super smart um uh Lightning Fast research assistant it doesn't come up with insights it doesn't come up with insights it doesn't and I fed it situations where I had an Insight like around the Princeton Review"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=1980s)

ChatGPT and similar tools are research assistants that can't make original insights. Their real value is helping you ask better questions—like a cello in the hands of Yo-Yo Ma versus an amateur. Mastery comes from learning what questions to ask.

### Better Questions Create Better Choices
> "instead of asking how do i x... instead of asking how do I ask the following question who knows how to do whatever that is so all of a sudden instead of my trying to figure out how I Adam ought to do it... I've got lots of choices now"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=2160s)

When you reframe 'How do I do X?' into 'Who knows how to do X?', you instantly expand your options. Good questions increase the number of choices available to you, giving you more flexibility to find optimal solutions.

### Learn the Right Lesson from Others' Mistakes
> "what he concluded that none of them had lost their instruments he concluded they were publicity stunts he so could not imagine that somebody... he should have said is if they lost theirs under what circumstances might I lose mine that's not the lesson he drove"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=4320s)

When observing others' failures, most people conclude vague lessons ('be more careful'). The deeper lesson requires imagining yourself in similar circumstances and asking: 'Under what conditions might this happen to me?' Avoid dismissing warnings as inapplicable to your situation.

### Solve the Root Problem, Not the Symptom
> "so that uh a um and they bring in an architect and he he says oh you don't have to speed up the elevators just put mirrors in in every waiting area... on problem solved didn't have to speed up the elevators"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TqQzgmLqK2I&t=2640s)

The elevator complaint problem wasn't slow elevators—it was boredom. Adding mirrors didn't speed elevators but gave people something to do. Identify why people are actually complaining before investing in solutions; reframe the issue to offer choices rather than just distraction.

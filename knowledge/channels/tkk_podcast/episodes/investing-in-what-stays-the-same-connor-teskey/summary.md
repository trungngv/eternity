# Investing in What Stays the Same | Connor Teskey

## Lessons

### The 90% Certainty Rule Beats Perfection
> "when something feels 90% right, you do that transaction or you do that deal and the most important thing is you do 10 of them. And you're going to be right nine times out of 10."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=xSwHXjH8Og4&t=540s)

When something feels 90% right, execute the deal. Doing 10 deals with 90% conviction yields better results than waiting for impossible certainty. Perfect models create paralysis rather than better decisions.

### De-Risk Through Contractual Certainty, Not Perfection
> "we do not like to put capital in the ground unless we lock in our CAPEX contract, our off-take contract, our EPC contract, and our financing contract all at once."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=xSwHXjH8Og4&t=960s)

Lock in all critical deal variables simultaneously—CAPEX, revenue contracts, construction contracts, and financing—to remove market risk while accepting execution risk. This creates inflation-protected cash flows.

### Communication Skills Matter as Much as Analysis
> "it's not just the ability to do the work, but explain the work. And if you can't do both, you know, it's kind of irrelevant."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=xSwHXjH8Og4&t=900s)

Technical excellence means nothing if stakeholders can't understand it. The ability to explain work clearly is equally important as the ability to do the work—without both, impact is lost.

### Geographic Distance Forces Initiative and Ownership
> "there is an incredible forcing function of not working in the same office as your boss... when you begin to take the initiative and do those things on your own... you actually have a much higher shooting percentage than you expect."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=xSwHXjH8Og4&t=600s)

Working remotely from leadership eliminates the crutch of asking permission. Forced independence builds confidence, accelerates decision-making, and reveals hidden capability in people who otherwise wouldn't step up.

### Invest in Assets That Stay Necessary Forever
> "focus on high-quality assets that make up the backbone of the global economy, you know, critical assets or services that really drive the growth and productivity of the communities."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=xSwHXjH8Og4&t=300s)

Focus exclusively on high-quality assets critical to the global economy's backbone. These assets have durable demand, strong downside protection, and remain relevant across economic cycles.

### Culture Built on Putting Others First Scales Indefinitely
> "one of the big cultural aspects of Brookfield is almost worry about putting others in a position to succeed more more than yourself."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=xSwHXjH8Og4&t=120s)

Organizations where leaders prioritize others' success over personal credit compound over decades. This creates a meritocratic environment where talent thrives and succession happens naturally.

### Availability Signals Commitment More Than Hours Worked
> "being available for other people on the team... I almost think it's the availability that people perceive as working hard."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=xSwHXjH8Og4&t=780s)

Being accessible to colleagues for questions, advice, and collaboration is often perceived as hard work more than visibly grinding on tasks. It's a differentiator that multiplies your impact through others.

### Build for Downside; Upside Takes Care of Itself
> "if you buy high-quality businesses in good markets that have strong downside protection, if you underwrite the worst-case scenario really, really well, the base case will end up being very attractive."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=xSwHXjH8Og4&t=1800s)

Focus investment diligence entirely on worst-case scenarios and downside protection. If the downside is protected through quality and structure, attractive base-case returns and unexpected upside follow naturally.

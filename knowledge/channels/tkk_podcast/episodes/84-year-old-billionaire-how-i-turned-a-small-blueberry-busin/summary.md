# 84-Year-Old Billionaire: How I Turned a Small Blueberry Business into a Global Empire | John Bragg
**Source:** [Watch on YouTube](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM)
**Date:** --

## Lessons

### Commit fully and execute on your decision
> "I'd already made a decision I was going to be in the blueberry business. So I built a freezing plant in 1968 when I was 28 and didn't have any idea what I was doing."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=0s)

Once you decide on a direction, commit completely to it even without full knowledge or experience. This unwavering commitment provides the foundation for learning and success.

### Honor your word—it's your foundation
> "If you tell somebody you're going to do something, you're going to do it. And if you say you're going to be there at 7, you're going to be there at 7 or before."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=120s)

Keeping promises and showing up on time builds trust and differentiates you in business. This principle, learned from parents, became central to survival in small communities and success in business.

### Strengthen competitors by sharing knowledge
> "if it's good for us, why don't we share it with the farmers? A lot of them deal with us and bring us their fruit. If we can make them more efficient, that keeps us in business."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=600s)

Sharing research and improvements with competitors and farmers benefits the entire industry ecosystem and keeps suppliers healthy and engaged. This creates a sustainable advantage rather than a zero-sum game.

### Look at the horizon, not your feet
> "There's a famous quote by I think it was D Hammershaw who said that that only those who look at the horizon find the right road. If you look at your at your feet, you'll stumble."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=1320s)

Long-term thinking and vision matter more than short-term obstacles. By focusing on distant goals rather than immediate difficulties, you find the right path forward.

### Be the lowest-cost, highest-quality producer
> "You have to be the lowcost producer and you have to be top quality. and top quality doesn't necessarily cost. In fact, quite often it's cheaper."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=1740s)

In commodity businesses, there's no substitute for being both the cheapest and the best. Quality and cost efficiency are not trade-offs—they often reinforce each other.

### Build a culture of endless improvement
> "we strive every day and we have a culture of every year trying to do it better. There's always changes we can make in the manufacturing line or on the farm."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=1800s)

Success requires a daily commitment to doing things better. Create an organizational culture where everyone is open to change and improvement, identifying areas to enhance every single year.

### Focus obsessively on one product
> "tried to find a product we could run and run the hell out of it. As we would say, run the hell out of it."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=1440s)

Success comes from mastering a single product and scaling it relentlessly rather than chasing multiple opportunities. Disciplined focus prevents the dilution that ruins many successful entrepreneurs.

### Most fortunes are built after age 50
> "Most of my real equity, the way you would measure it, has come after 60. But before that, I was building land bases and factories and people."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=4560s)

Early career is about building foundations—people, assets, factories, and relationships. The real wealth creation comes decades later when you leverage that accumulated base with focus and leverage.

### Success is developing people, not accumulating money
> "Success for me is is the development of a great team. We have homegrown leaders that are competing with the with the world and doing it very well."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=iSGkxcEL_lM&t=5100s)

The ultimate measure of business success is building a great team and developing leaders who can add value. This is more meaningful than wealth accumulation and more durable than any single transaction.

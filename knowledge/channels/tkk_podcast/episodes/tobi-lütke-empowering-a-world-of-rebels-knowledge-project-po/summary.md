## Lessons

### Saying yes means saying no to everything else
> "to say yes to a thing you actually say no to every other thing you could have done with this period of time"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=0s)

When you commit to one thing, you're choosing against all other possible uses of that time. This principle is fundamental to strategy—every yes has an implicit cost of forgoing alternatives. Understanding this forces intentional prioritization rather than attempting to do everything.

### Protect your mission during periods of chaos
> "the most important thing is to keep most of one thing the most important thing in our case is like make a product that's really appreciate about entrepreneurs"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=122s)

In volatile times, the core reason your company exists becomes more important to defend, not abandon. By keeping focus on your mission even when reality changes, you can adapt tactics while maintaining strategic direction and meaning for your team.

### Best practices mean average risk adjustment
> "best practices actually just simply means don't take risk and do with what everyone else is saying you should be doing"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=1160s)

Following industry best practices is a way to avoid responsibility for outcomes by doing what everyone else does. True innovation requires departing from best practices—and accepting personal accountability for results, whether they outperform or underperform.

### Founders have unique legitimacy to cut deeply
> "I could do this is because I could draw on the legitimacy of like hey I I have seen every single version of his company"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=960s)

A founder's story and stake in the company give them a 'legitimacy account' they can draw on to make bold changes others cannot. This allows cutting 60% of initiatives (as Tobi did) without losing organizational trust—a capability that's nearly impossible for non-founder leaders.

### Subtraction is harder and more valuable
> "the set of things that can be done it becomes smaller and smaller smaller and a set of things that has to be maintained gets larger and larger and larger"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=900s)

Adding features and initiatives feels productive but accumulates unsustainable complexity. Removing things requires courage and legitimacy, yet it's often what unlocks breakthrough performance and organizational clarity.

### Infinite games require different decision-making
> "if you are on an infinite Journey change is actually welcome change is change is actually welcome change is clarifying changes a new bit of information"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=7320s)

Many pursuits (business, learning, life) are infinite games with no finish line, unlike finite games with winners and losers. In infinite games, change is clarifying rather than threatening, and you continuously adapt without trying to 'win'—you keep playing.

### Create psychological safety to enable risk-taking
> "it's not failure it's not like it's like it's a successful discovery of something that didn't work if if you underperform it's it's we know actually more data"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=1380s)

Teams innovate by attempting things that might fail. Creating an environment where failure is treated as 'successful discovery' rather than shame removes the paralyzing fear that prevents experimentation and learning.

### Align all stakeholders to your core mission
> "you have share order Community sometimes Society sometimes um you're definitely your local economies your employees your customers"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=600s)

Organizations succeed by bringing diverse stakeholder groups—employees, customers, communities, boards—into genuine alignment with your mission. This requires honest communication and reciprocal obligation, not top-down mandate.

### Sleep quality comes from behavioral change, not hacks
> "by day three except for a night I have been sleeping perfectly ever since I mean once a month I maybe like"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=7940s)

Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) is more effective than sleeping pills or optimization tricks because it addresses the false stories we tell ourselves about sleep. The key is retraining your body's natural sleep signal through consistent behavior.

### Apply systems thinking from engineering to strategy
> "I'm not putting engineering skills on speed dial anymore they are now the core of how I make decisions"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hUug8tEWtoY&t=8580s)

The detailed, iterative, systems-thinking mindset that works in engineering—understanding layers, testing assumptions, optimizing for constraints—is equally powerful for organizational strategy and leadership decision-making. It grounds intuition in rigor.

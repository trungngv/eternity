# How Belichick Actually Makes Decisions (It’s Not What You Think)
**Source:** [Watch on YouTube](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs)
**Date:** --

## Lessons

### Preparation Success Price Paid in Advance
> "The big thing about preparation success is the price has to be paid in advance. You have to put in the work in advance. You have to put in the work before you get any results."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=0s)

Excellence requires doing the work before you see results. Waiting to prepare once results matter is too late—all foundational work must happen beforehand through practice, study, and conditioning.

### Four Cornerstones: Do Your Job, Work Hard, Be Attentive, Put Team First
> "Do your job, work hard, be attentive, and put the team first"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=60s)

These four principles form the foundation of excellence and were displayed daily in the Patriots facility. They apply universally to every role and every person, from coaching to equipment management to playing.

### Hard Work Requires Accomplishment, Not Just Presence
> "If you don't really accomplish anything, then that really is not not working hard. It's actually we refer to it as a day off."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=120s)

Simply showing up and going through the motions—what Belichick calls 'eyewash'—is not hard work. True hard work produces measurable improvement and accomplishment toward specific goals.

### Work Ethic Can Overcome Talent Limitations
> "Guys with a great work ethic and who really are committed to improving can catch a lot of players with more talent who don't work as hard."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=240s)

Sustained effort and commitment to improvement over time can allow less talented players to eventually outperform those with greater natural abilities who don't maintain discipline. Talent gets you through high school and college, but consistency wins in the NFL.

### You Cannot Win Until You Stop Losing
> "You cannot win until you keep from losing, which reminds me of inversion from Charlie Munger. Anything that prevents you from achieving your best that you control—so it's not the opponent, it's something that happens internally."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=720s)

Most games are lost rather than won—they're lost through self-inflicted errors like penalties, turnovers, and poor execution that you control. Eliminate these internal mistakes before focusing on beating the opponent.

### Internal Team Relationships Matter More Than External Validation
> "The guys that are most important to each of us are the guys in that locker room. You know, the guys that you win and lose with and live and die with each day. What's more important is what the guy next to you thinks about you. That's the one that matters the most."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=1140s)

What matters most is the respect, trust, and communication you build with your immediate teammates, not social media metrics or external opinions. These relationships are earned daily through preparation and reliability.

### Understanding Every Organizational Role Enables Better Leadership
> "I shot film. I lined the fields. I moved equipment out there. I ran off Xerox copies. I made notebooks. I sharpened pencil. It really helped me because I understood how everything functioned, and what was hard about those jobs and what wasn't hard about those jobs."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=2520s)

Starting at the bottom and doing all the unglamorous work—shooting film, moving equipment, managing logistics—teaches you how the entire organization functions. This foundation prevents you from making unrealistic demands as a leader.

### Discipline is Doing the Right Thing Over and Over
> "Discipline is doing the right thing over and over again. Doing it every time. It's just continuing to do it every time."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=2940s)

Discipline isn't a momentary act of willpower—it's the consistent, repetitive execution of excellence every single time, whether anyone is watching or not. It's the shopping cart test applied to every aspect of life.

### Manage Expectations by Focusing Only on the Present
> "Manage expectations. The expectations are to have a good day today, have a good week, and to win this game. Those are our weekly expectations, not where we're going to be in January, who we're going to play in the playoffs."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=3960s)

Avoid predictions and long-term projections that create distractions. Focus expectations only on what you can control this week—having a good day, preparing well, winning the immediate game ahead.

### Control of Game Execution Matters More Than Score
> "Sometimes you play a game and you feel like you have control of the game, but you don't have control of the score. In that Super Bowl, we felt like we had pretty good control of the game, but we didn't have control of the score at all."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=HHQsrLPUXKs&t=4080s)

Sometimes you play well and control the game's flow but lose due to unlucky breaks, while other times the score masks poor performance. Focus on executing your gameplan correctly rather than obsessing over the scoreboard.

# Tom Gayner: Lessons That Every Investor Needs to Learn (Audio)

## Lessons

### Deserve what you want through relentless effort
> "the best way to get what you want is to deserve what you want and working backwards from that idea and that concept"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=240s)

The foundation of success is becoming worthy of your goals through consistent self-improvement and hard work. This principle of earning rather than expecting creates a sustainable path to achievement.

### Always help others; relationships compound over time
> "anytime anytime you can do a favor for somebody do it just do it uh because life is long and you never know how those things come back over time"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=300s)

Consistently doing favors and adding value to others creates a long-term network effect. The universe tends to reward those who operate with genuine helpfulness as their default.

### Build win-win-win business ecosystems
> "you want companies that are doing things for their customers rather than to their customers"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=720s)

Sustainable success requires creating businesses where customers, employees, and the company all benefit. Short-term extraction always fails when compared to long-term value creation.

### Obsess about opportunity cost in decisions
> "if we do that what are we not going to do and you have to make a choice"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=1680s)

Every choice forecloses other possibilities. Constantly asking 'what are we not doing?' develops the intellectual discipline needed to make superior decisions.

### Understand what you reject and why
> "he didn't want to only just understand what you were doing and why you were doing it but he wanted to understand what you weren't doing and why you weren't doing it"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=5880s)

Good leaders don't just explain their choices; they articulate their constraints and reasoning. This transparency builds trust and psychological safety with teams.

### Judge yourself by process quality, not outcome
> "if if you're judging Yourself by the outcome in the short run you you're going to make more mistakes than you otherwise would"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=5940s)

Outcomes are influenced by luck and factors outside your control. A sound process compounds over time and is the only metric worth measuring yourself against.

### Avoiding stupidity beats chasing brilliance
> "avoiding stupidity is easier than seeking Brilliance"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=5820s)

Most financial and business success comes from systematically avoiding obvious mistakes, not from clever insights. Simple discipline outperforms complexity.

### Organize with small autonomous decision units
> "generally speaking almost every single decision is made by a small group of people who have accountability to one another"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=5640s)

Large organizations stay nimble through decentralized decision-making in small teams with accountability. This structure prevents bureaucracy and enables rapid learning.

### Low interest rates remove discipline
> "by the time the curfew gets to midnight or past midnight that is effectively no curfew and as one comedian once joked he thought they should uh turn off ATM machines after midnight because no possible good comes from getting cash out"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=6180s)

When cost of capital approaches zero, bad ideas get funded alongside good ones. Historical interest rate discipline forced thoughtful capital allocation that loose money destroys.

### Compound returns by doing more of what works
> "the great algorithm in life is do more of what's working so so I bought the first share of Burkshire in 1990"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=vRXW9FUxfjU&t=7200s)

The simple algorithm is to identify what's working and do more of it. Patience to let winners compound matters far more than seeking novelty.

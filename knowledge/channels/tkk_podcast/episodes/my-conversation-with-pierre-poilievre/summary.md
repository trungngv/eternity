# My conversation with Pierre Poilievre
**Source:** [Watch on YouTube](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4)
**Date:** --

## Lessons

### Canadian Promise: Opportunity through hard work and merit
> "I was adopted by a couple of school teachers and yet I've been able to make it here. And the story is the same for my wife. She came here as a refugee from Venezuela and she's been able to have a great life. We believe that this is a country where you can start anywhere and get anywhere."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4&t=150s)

True national unity comes from believing that anyone, regardless of background, can achieve success in Canada through hard work. This shared belief in meritocracy is what binds Canadians together and distinguishes the country.

### Judge people by character, not identity group
> "We judge them as people as individuals on their personal character and conduct rather than their gender, their race, their religion. I think it's better to treat everyone like an individual and give them a chance. You're Canadian first above all our other identities."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4&t=270s)

Identity politics divides citizens into hostile camps. Instead, treating all people as individuals judged by their personal conduct creates social cohesion and prevents the sectarian violence increasingly seen in Canada.

### Big government transfers wealth to the rich, not poor
> "When government grows beyond a basic social safety net, it actually doesn't benefit low-income people, it starts to massively transfer wealth from the working class to the extremely rich. It's kind of like a taking from the have nots to give to the have yachts."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4&t=1290s)

Counterintuitively, expanding government beyond a basic social safety net actually harms the working class by creating inflation, funding consultants, and enriching the wealthy. Sound money and lower taxes are what help struggling families most.

### Slow bureaucratic permitting kills economic growth
> "We have the second slowest building permits in the OECD. It takes 17 years to get a mine approved, three times longer to get approval for a housing development in Canada versus the UK or the US."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4&t=1770s)

Canada's multi-year permitting processes drive investment to faster jurisdictions like the US and Mexico. Streamlining approvals to months instead of years—while maintaining environmental standards—is critical to competitiveness.

### Working people have better judgment than credentialed elites
> "I talk to truck drivers and mechanics and farmers and I find the latter group is actually smarter. Mr. Carney, for example, told us in 2021, don't worry about inflation. There won't be any inflation. Well, he was totally wrong."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4&t=1140s)

Truck drivers, farmers, and mechanics understand economic reality better than credentialed experts in government. Humility about expert knowledge matters; ordinary citizens often make better predictions about inflation and policy outcomes.

### Catch-and-release laws enable serial offenders
> "In Vancouver, the police had to arrest the same 40 offenders 6,000 times. Each one of these guys was arrested on average 150 times a year. You take those 40 guys off the street, you eliminate 6,000 crimes."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4&t=3420s)

Liberal bail laws that automatically release even repeat violent criminals create a revolving-door system where small numbers of prolific offenders commit thousands of crimes, destroying public safety and business confidence.

### Government funding corrupts media independence
> "Many Canadian media outlets are partially government-funded. This makes it very difficult for media to make a judgment without keeping in the back of their head, which politician is going to pay the biggest subsidy. I don't think government should control the media."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4&t=2970s)

When government funds media through subsidies and advertising, outlets face inherent pressure to favor whoever promises the most support. This structural conflict of interest undermines journalism's ability to hold all politicians accountable.

### Tax incentives should reward domestic investment
> "I want to pass the Canada first reinvestment tax cut: zero capital gains when you reinvest in Canada. Then you get that tax deferral. But if you take the money out of Canada, you don't."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=hS-xso4Nmo4&t=780s)

A reinvestment tax cut that gives zero capital gains tax when profits are invested in Canada—but not elsewhere—redirects billions in capital away from the US and creates a powerful incentive for business expansion at home.

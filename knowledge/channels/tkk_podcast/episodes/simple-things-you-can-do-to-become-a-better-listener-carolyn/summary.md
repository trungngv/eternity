## Lessons

### Listening Tends to Be Contagious
> "listening tends to be contagious so how each of us listens does tend to wear off on other people"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TNyaaUF0hwE&t=0s)

How we listen directly influences those around us, creating ripples throughout relationships and teams. Our listening patterns become a model others unconsciously adopt, making it one of the most powerful leadership tools.

### Deep Listening Creates Extraordinary Human Connection
> "when people feel really truly seen and heard it's one of the most extraordinary experiences that a person can have"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TNyaaUF0hwE&t=1s)

When people feel truly seen and heard through genuine listening, it becomes one of the most transformative experiences available. This deep listening is actually a prerequisite for learning to listen well.

### Language Shapes More Than It Expresses
> "our language is not just an expression of our meaning making but it also shapes our meaning making"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TNyaaUF0hwE&t=7s)

Our words are not merely reflections of how we think—they actively construct our meaning-making. By changing our language, we can reshape how we understand ourselves and our responsibilities.

### Shifting from Have-To to Want-To Reclaims Agency
> "when I change the words to want to it really really changed the way I made sense of my relationship to my responsibility"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TNyaaUF0hwE&t=8s)

Replacing obligatory language with choice language fundamentally changes our relationship to responsibility. This small linguistic shift creates felt agency and transforms obligation into conscious commitment.

### Identity Statements Create Separating Hierarchies
> "I would never do that I'm not the sort of person who would do that... so that sort of that reinforces my sense of myself as a person"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TNyaaUF0hwE&t=23s)

Saying "I would never do that" unconsciously places us above others in an arbitrary hierarchy, severing connection. These identity statements protect our self-image but limit our ability to relate to different perspectives.

### Check-Ins Create System Awareness and Trust
> "we start every meeting with the check-in... it's a system scanning exercise right so the check-in someone poses a question and every person in the room responds"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TNyaaUF0hwE&t=37s)

Starting meetings with a check-in where everyone responds to one question creates a scanning exercise that reveals patterns and builds connection. This simple practice proves more powerful than agenda-driven meetings.

### Building Somatic Awareness Connects Mind and Body
> "where do you feel that in your body... to turn your attention inward and to see if you can scan and notice if there's any connection"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TNyaaUF0hwE&t=63s)

Learning to notice where emotions and sensations appear in your body creates crucial feedback loops. This practice bridges the gap between abstract thinking and embodied wisdom that guides better decisions.

### Ask "How Could I Be Wrong?" to Expand Perspective
> "the question how could I be wrong assumes that when it comes to the perspective of another person we probably are very wrong right"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=TNyaaUF0hwE&t=82s)

This question is a habit that directly increases curiosity and prompts genuine perspective-taking. It assumes our understanding is incomplete and invites the wisdom that only others' different viewpoints can provide.

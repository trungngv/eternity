## Lessons

### Life is fundamentally a game to play with intensity
> "The great game is life. And we play many games in life. Everything is a game. And game suggests something that's not serious, but of course games are intensely serious."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=0s)

Life itself is the great game, encompassing career, relationships, business, and more. Games are intensely serious endeavors that demand focused commitment, making this framing essential for approaching life with the right mindset.

### Connecting with others is the real magic in life
> "the real magic is connecting with others. That if you want to do anything in the world, and this is as true personally as well as professionally, it's all about creating a vision for others to join."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=420s)

Creating and sharing a compelling vision that brings others together is the most powerful force available. Whether in business or personal relationships, enrolling others in your vision is how meaningful things happen.

### Create fun and delight for everyone you meet
> "the second thing I've learned is the importance of creating fun and delight for the other no matter what you're doing in any situation."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=240s)

Making others feel joy and excitement transforms interactions from transactional to magical. This simple practice shifts any situation—interviews become games, conversations become collaborations—and naturally brings out the best in people.

### Technology hijacks attention, fueling loneliness epidemics
> "technology is designed to uh hijack your attention. Right? That's what technology is designed to do... All of that's engineered. And it's resulted in a hijacking of attention."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=600s)

Modern technology is engineered to capture and monetize your attention through confirmation bias and emotional manipulation. This hijacking of our most valuable resource directly contributes to the rising loneliness crisis, especially among young people.

### Happiness is a byproduct of full engagement
> "Happiness is something that catches us by surprise. It's a byproduct, right? I would say, 'I don't know. I'm so engaged in this interview that I have no idea actually how I'm feeling.'"
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=1080s)

Pursuing happiness directly paradoxically prevents it. Happiness arrives unbidden when you're completely absorbed in a task or interaction, making presence and engagement the true path to well-being.

### Questions you ask shape the world you see
> "the questions that you ask of the world shape what you see."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=1440s)

Your internal questions act as filters for reality. By shifting from "How do I impress this person?" to "How do I create joy for them?" you fundamentally transform your perception and outcomes.

### Anomalies and surprises reveal truth and opportunity
> "What surprise is telling you, Shane, is this. Your model of the world is incorrect, which is fantastic. It's a learning opportunity."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=2220s)

When something doesn't make sense or surprises you, it signals your mental model is incomplete. Rather than dismiss it, investigate the anomaly—this is where real learning and investment opportunities hide.

### Work ethic determines success far more than talent
> "The better belief is that your success is determined by how hard you work. And then it's just a matter of choice. If you want something, work for it, and you will if you want it."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=2520s)

Intelligence and innate ability matter far less than willingness to work. Success isn't mysterious—it's a direct function of effort applied. Believing this shifts you from victim to agent.

### Parents must model struggle, not shield children from it
> "something that parents can do that I think would be a great lesson for their young children is to model struggling with something, to model struggle, and to model staying with the struggle. Not giving up."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=2640s)

Children learn resilience and growth mindset not through words but through watching adults wrestle with difficulty and persist. Schools and society often hide struggle, leaving young people unprepared for life's inevitable challenges.

### Continuous experimentation beats rigid repetition of failures
> "why don't you experiment till you find something that works? And then once you find that... It once took me 2 years, 2 years mind you, of experimenting every single day several times with different students to find a way to express a certain concept."
[Watch this moment →](https://youtube.com/watch?v=7BfKEkEkKlk&t=3120s)

When something isn't working, don't repeat it harder—experiment systematically until you find what works. Once found, stick with the script. Most people fail by doing more of the same instead of trying radically different approaches.

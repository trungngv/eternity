Canadian Promise: Opportunity through hard work and merit

"I was adopted by a couple of school teachers and yet I've been able to make it here. And the story is the same for my wife. She came here as a refugee from Venezuela and she's been able to have a great life. We believe that this is a country where you can start anywhere and get anywhere." Watch this moment →

True national unity comes from believing that anyone, regardless of background, can achieve success in Canada through hard work. This shared belief in meritocracy is what binds Canadians together and distinguishes the country.

Judge people by character, not identity group

"We judge them as people as individuals on their personal character and conduct rather than their gender, their race, their religion. I think it's better to treat everyone like an individual and give them a chance. You're Canadian first above all our other identities." Watch this moment →

Identity politics divides citizens into hostile camps. Instead, treating all people as individuals judged by their personal conduct creates social cohesion and prevents the sectarian violence increasingly seen in Canada.

Big government transfers wealth to the rich, not poor

"When government grows beyond a basic social safety net, it actually doesn't benefit low-income people, it starts to massively transfer wealth from the working class to the extremely rich. It's kind of like a taking from the have nots to give to the have yachts." Watch this moment →

Counterintuitively, expanding government beyond a basic social safety net actually harms the working class by creating inflation, funding consultants, and enriching the wealthy. Sound money and lower taxes are what help struggling families most.

Slow bureaucratic permitting kills economic growth

"We have the second slowest building permits in the OECD. It takes 17 years to get a mine approved, three times longer to get approval for a housing development in Canada versus the UK or the US." Watch this moment →

Canada's multi-year permitting processes drive investment to faster jurisdictions like the US and Mexico. Streamlining approvals to months instead of years—while maintaining environmental standards—is critical to competitiveness.

Working people have better judgment than credentialed elites

"I talk to truck drivers and mechanics and farmers and I find the latter group is actually smarter. Mr. Carney, for example, told us in 2021, don't worry about inflation. There won't be any inflation. Well, he was totally wrong." Watch this moment →

Truck drivers, farmers, and mechanics understand economic reality better than credentialed experts in government. Humility about expert knowledge matters; ordinary citizens often make better predictions about inflation and policy outcomes.

Catch-and-release laws enable serial offenders

"In Vancouver, the police had to arrest the same 40 offenders 6,000 times. Each one of these guys was arrested on average 150 times a year. You take those 40 guys off the street, you eliminate 6,000 crimes." Watch this moment →

Liberal bail laws that automatically release even repeat violent criminals create a revolving-door system where small numbers of prolific offenders commit thousands of crimes, destroying public safety and business confidence.

Government funding corrupts media independence

"Many Canadian media outlets are partially government-funded. This makes it very difficult for media to make a judgment without keeping in the back of their head, which politician is going to pay the biggest subsidy. I don't think government should control the media." Watch this moment →

When government funds media through subsidies and advertising, outlets face inherent pressure to favor whoever promises the most support. This structural conflict of interest undermines journalism's ability to hold all politicians accountable.

Tax incentives should reward domestic investment

"I want to pass the Canada first reinvestment tax cut: zero capital gains when you reinvest in Canada. Then you get that tax deferral. But if you take the money out of Canada, you don't." Watch this moment →

A reinvestment tax cut that gives zero capital gains tax when profits are invested in Canada—but not elsewhere—redirects billions in capital away from the US and creates a powerful incentive for business expansion at home.

Private ownership frees businesses from market distraction

Motivation as Internal Pull, Not External Push

"motivation is a pull it is something that like is internal that fuels you that drives you and a number of the people that I tend to work with are very obsessed" Watch this moment →

Motivation is an internal force that fuels high performers, while discipline requires constant pushing. True excellence comes from being pulled by obsession rather than pushed by willpower, making motivation the more sustainable and powerful force for peak performance.

Internal Drive Cannot Be Taught or Created

"internal drive is something you can't teach uh and I'm very you know perhaps that goes against current wisdom I do think that as long as someone has that internal hunger and that internal drive" Watch this moment →

You cannot instill hunger or drive in someone who doesn't possess it naturally. However, you can align existing internal drivers with opportunities and compelling vision to unlock motivation in those who have the foundational hunger.

Imaginary Rules Limit Your Perceived Potential

"we are also simultaneously ingesting other rules that are unspoken and that are really unsubstantiated right like what is possible for us um you know what are the things that we can do what are we capable of" Watch this moment →

We unconsciously absorb unspoken, unsubstantiated 'rules' about what's possible for us from our environment and upbringing. These invisible beliefs constrain our potential because we never question them, preventing us from seeing opportunities that actually exist beyond our perceived limits.

Choose Survivor Mindset Over Victim Mentality

"you can be a victim or you can be a Survivor and those are two very different cognitive positions you know you can't control what happens to you in either circumstance but one is very powerful" Watch this moment →

When facing adversity, you can cognitively choose to see yourself as a victim or survivor—both face the same circumstances, but this mental choice determines your future. While you cannot control what happens, you control how you interpret and respond to it.

Boundaries Create Freedom and Peak Performance

"boundaries do free us and they allow us to live our best life but we have to really enforce them and we have to be really clear on what they are" Watch this moment →

Setting clear boundaries around time, commitments, and energy allocation actually liberates you to operate at your highest capacity. Boundaries enable focus on your areas of highest and best use, making them essential for both productivity and well-being.

Build Resilience With Multiple Life Foundations

"if you have only one leg of a stool it's easy to kick out right so like if you say hey everything that I am is my business it's when your business does poorly or it collapses it just shakes you" Watch this moment →

Like a single-legged stool that's easy to topple, putting your entire identity in one area (like work) is fragile. Multiple stable foundations—relationships, health, spirituality, career—create resilience so that struggles in one area don't collapse your entire life.

Small Wins Build Compounding Confidence

"if you can do small things challenge yourself in some small way where you're going to take a risk and go outside of yourself it translates and when you start having some wins you will start to pull that mindset in your work" Watch this moment →

Taking calculated risks in low-stakes environments builds swagger and confidence that transfers to bigger challenges. Each small victory proves to yourself that you can execute, making the next risk easier and creating a positive momentum spiral.

Keep Goals Private, Share Only Results

"when you share your goals and people give you praise it actually pulls from motivation to achieve them so it's why vision boards aren't really all that great for you" Watch this moment →

Publicly announcing goals actually reduces motivation because you receive a dopamine reward from praise before doing the work. Keeping goals private while sharing only results preserves motivation and allows you to build credibility through demonstrated achievement.
